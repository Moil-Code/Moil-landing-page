Playbook 02 · Client delivery

The Moil client SEO and AEO playbook

A repeatable 90-day system for small local and service businesses — run by the owner, in 45 minutes a week, against competitors who never claimed the free profile that decides a third of the outcome.

For Moil delivery team + SMB clients Review quarterly Updated 2026-08-13

What separates this from Playbook 01

Playbook 01 optimizes one B2B SaaS site for national and bilingual demand. This is a delivery system: the same sequence, run over and over, for clients who are typically 1–20-person local service businesses — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, landscaping, cleaning, restaurants, salons, auto, home services — often bilingual, often Hispanic-owned, and almost always with no marketing plan at all. The gap between the two playbooks is one word: local.

Design constraint

The client is the owner, and the owner has 45 minutes a week. Every deliverable below either runs inside Moil — Content360, the plan, the coach, Smart Hiring — or is a one-page checklist. If a step needs an agency to execute, it does not belong in this playbook. That is the whole point of the $25/mo versus $3,000–$8,000/mo story.

02Intake and diagnostic

Week 0. Nothing gets built until these ten things are known and the baseline is scored.

Data to collect before anything else

  • Legal business name, exact address, phone, hours — this becomes the NAP standard
  • Google Business Profile: claimed? verified? who has the login?
  • Website URL, platform, who can edit it
  • Service area: cities, ZIPs, radius
  • Top 3 services by profit, not by volume
  • Top 3 local competitors by name
  • Current review count and average rating, per platform
  • Languages the business actually serves customers in
  • Existing analytics: GA4, Search Console, call tracking — anything

Non-negotiable

That last item. Without average ticket and close rate we cannot report in dollars, and SMB owners rightly disengage from reports denominated in impressions.

03Stop losing leads already earned

No content, no links, no strategy decks. Just the fundamentals, in this order.

3.1 Google Business Profile — always first

  1. Claim and verify. If the login is lost, run reclamation before anything else.
  2. Primary category. The #1 individual Local Pack factor. Pick the most specific accurate one — "HVAC contractor" beats "contractor", "Mexican restaurant" beats "restaurant". Check what the top-3 map competitors use, then pick the one matching what the business actually does most.
  3. Secondary categories. Every accurate one, no stuffing. Inaccurate categories get profiles suspended.
  4. Services, each with a 2–3 sentence description containing the service name and the city. This text feeds AI Overviews on local queries directly.
  5. Business description, 750 characters: what, who for, which cities, languages served, one proof point.
  6. Photos: 10+ real ones — team, trucks, before/after, storefront, work in progress. No stock.
  7. Hours, including holiday hours. Wrong hours is the most common cause of a bad first review.
  8. Service areas set to real cities served, not a 200-mile fantasy radius.
  9. Attributes: "Spanish spoken", women-owned, veteran-owned, Latino-owned — set every true one. These are filterable and they surface in AI answers about the business.
  10. Messaging and booking on, only if the owner will actually respond within an hour.
  11. Weekly Google Post — Content360 can generate these from the client's content calendar.
Bilingual note

GBP is one profile, one language. Put the primary market's language in the description, and cover the second language via the "Spanish spoken" attribute, Spanish reviews, and a Spanish page on the site.

3.2 NAP consistency

Even minor Name/Address/Phone discrepancies suppress local rankings. Write the one canonical NAP string in the client file, then make these ten match it byte-for-byte:

Google Business Profile · website footer · Apple Business Connect · Bing Places · Facebook · Yelp · Better Business Bureau · industry directory (Angi / Thumbtack / HomeAdvisor) · local Chamber of Commerce · Hispanic Chamber or local Latino business directory

Chamber and EDC listings are doubly valuable — Moil already holds those partnerships, so they are a real delivery advantage, not a cold outreach task.

3.3 Review engine — and the 150 threshold

Below roughly 150 reviews per location, AI assistants rarely name a business in local recommendations. This is the most under-appreciated fact in local AEO, and most SMBs are at 12.

The system, not the ask

  • Trigger the request at the moment of delight — job completed, invoice paid, tech leaving.
  • One channel, one link. SMS with the direct GBP review link out-converts everything else.
  • Ask by name, from a person, in the customer's language.
  • Respond to 100% of reviews within 48 hours. Responses are indexable text and a ranking signal — and the response is where the service name and city legitimately belong.
  • Never gate, never incentivize, never bulk-import. A review-gating penalty undoes a year of work.
  • Target: +8–15 reviews per month sustained, mixed EN/ES where the customer base is mixed.
Math to show the client on day one

At 12 reviews and +10/month they cross 150 in about 14 months — and cross the "competitive locally" line at roughly 50 in month four. Set that expectation immediately so month two doesn't feel like failure.

04Rankings, content and local AEO

4.1 Keyword set — small and honest

Local SMBs need about 20 terms, not 2,000. Four shapes:

ShapeExamplePage
service + cityac repair austinService page or service-area section
service + near meemergency plumber near meGBP + homepage
service + qualifier24 hour ac repair, same day drain cleaningService page section
questionhow much does ac repair cost in austin, why is my ac blowing warm airBlog / FAQ
Spanishplomero cerca de mí, reparación de aire acondicionado austinSpanish page

Follow khasky's rule: search the term first, look at what Google actually returns, and match the page type to the SERP. If the SERP is a map pack, the answer is GBP work — not a blog post.

Order of operations — questions before volume

Build the client’s Q&A list before touching a keyword tool. Ask the owner and their techs: what do customers actually ask on the phone? Those questions, in the customer’s own words, are the page structure. They are also almost always higher-intent than anything a volume tool surfaces — a customer asking “why is my AC blowing warm air and will it cost more than $500” is far closer to booking than one typing ac repair.

The 20-term set above then does exactly one job: choosing between two equally true phrasings, and catching demand nobody thought to answer. It never sets the agenda. Volume tells you how many people search a phrase; it cannot tell you which answer books the job. This is the same doctrine Playbook 01 runs on, and we hold clients to it because it is what works.

The Q&A build, per page

  1. Harvest 8–12 real questions per service page — from calls, texts, and what techs get asked on site. Verbatim, in the customer’s words, in both languages where the customer base is mixed.
  2. Answer each in 40–80 words under a question-shaped <h2>, self-contained enough to survive being lifted into an AI answer.
  3. State real numbers — a price range, a timeframe, a warranty length. Vague answers are unciteable.
  4. Reconcile with FAQPage schema: one source of truth between the visible Q&A and the JSON-LD.

4.2 Service-area pages without doorway spam

The temptation is 40 city pages from one template. Google's helpful-content systems target exactly that, and it fails.

The rule

Five real pages beat forty templated ones. A service-area page earns its URL only if it has at least three of: a named local job or case study · a photo taken in that city · a neighborhood or landmark reference that is actually true · city-specific pricing or permit and code notes · a review from a customer there · a genuine local nuance (hard water, coastal humidity, HOA rules, seasonal load).

Cadence: one city page per month, best city first. Expand only after the first one ranks.

4.3 Content — four pieces a month, generated in Moil

Content360 produces the 30-day calendar; the owner approves and adds the one thing AI cannot — photos of real work and one specific detail from a real job.

SlotTypePurpose
1Cost/pricing guide — how much does X cost in {city}Highest-intent question searches; the most-cited local content type
2Problem/diagnostic post — why is my X doing YTop of funnel, and genuinely useful in AI answers
3Job story / before-after with real photosProof, E-E-A-T, GBP post and social from one shoot
4Seasonal or local postFreshness and local relevance

Plus one GBP post weekly, and the month's reviews responded to.

Don’t let every client look identical

Flyer-style AI posts are effortless and they flood social — which is exactly the problem. Used daily, they make every brand look the same, and that generic look is what Content360’s varied templates exist to avoid. Two working rules: a flyer invites an action, daily content is an image plus a caption — don’t use one for the other. And rotate genuinely different templates (Addy has 40+) rather than reusing one. The differentiator that no competitor can copy is the client’s own photos of their own work, so every piece should carry at least one.

Publish the pricing content

Owners resist this. The counter-argument: AI assistants answer "how much does ac repair cost in Austin" constantly, and they cite the businesses that state ranges. Silence means a competitor's number gets quoted instead of ours.

4.4 Local AEO — how an SMB gets named by ChatGPT and Gemini

Local AI recommendations resolve mostly from entity strength, reviews and directory consensus — not from clever on-page copy. In priority order:

  1. Review volume and recency. The ~150 threshold. The dominant local AEO lever.
  2. Directory consensus. The same NAP, category and services repeated across 10+ sources is what the model reads as "this business exists and does this."
  3. Consistent facts. Hours, phone, services and languages identical everywhere. Contradiction means the model declines to state the fact.
  4. Extractable on-page answers. 40–60 word direct answers under question-shaped H2s. Service + city named explicitly in the first sentence.
  5. Third-party mentions. Local news, Chamber pages, supplier "find a dealer" pages, local blogs, local subreddit and Facebook-group mentions.
  6. LocalBusiness schema that matches the visible page exactly.
Skip for most clients

llms.txt. No measurable lift, and an SMB's 40 minutes a week is better spent on reviews. Add it only for clients with a real content library.

Client AEO test — monthly, 10 minutes

Ask each engine, in both languages where relevant:

"Best {service} in {city}"
"Who should I call for {problem} in {city}?"
"{Service} in {city} that speaks Spanish"
"Is {Business Name} any good?"
"How much does {service} cost in {city}?"

Log: named or not · what it said · who else got named. This screenshot is the single most persuasive slide in the monthly report — and when a client first sees themselves named, retention takes care of itself.

4.5 Paid, only where it earns its keep

Most of these clients should not run broad paid search. Three exceptions:

  1. Google Local Services Ads, where the vertical is eligible — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, cleaning, locksmith. Pay per lead, Google-guaranteed badge, sits above the map pack. Almost always the best first paid dollar for a service business.
  2. Brand and competitor-brand search, tiny budget, only once the site converts.
  3. Emergency and urgent terms where the job is high-ticket and same-day (emergency ac repair). Bid these during season only.

Everything else — display, broad awareness campaigns, boosted posts — is where SMB ad budgets die. Say no on the client's behalf. And per khasky: never use remarketing to mask a bad offer or a phone nobody answers.

06Bilingual delivery

Moil's structural advantage, and the most defensible part of the client offer. There are 5M+ Hispanic-owned US businesses generating roughly $800B a year, and Latino-owned employer businesses grew 35% from 2019–2024 against 6% nationally. Almost no local marketing provider serves them in Spanish properly.

Do

  • Real Spanish URLs — /es/servicios or /servicios — with reciprocal hreflang and x-default.
  • Spanish keyword research from scratch. Plomero vs. fontanero vs. plomería varies by region and community: ask the client which word their customers use, then verify against actual search results.
  • The GBP "Spanish spoken" attribute set, and a Spanish sentence in the business description where the customer base is majority Spanish-speaking.
  • Solicit reviews in the customer's language. Spanish reviews are what make a business surface for "que hable español" queries — in both classic search and AI answers.
  • Spanish content in the Content360 calendar from month one, not as a later phase.

Don't

  • Machine-translate and ship. Bad Spanish reads as untrustworthy to exactly the audience it targets.
  • Serve translated content on the same URL by cookie or JS swap. There must be a distinct, crawlable URL per language, or neither version ranks.
  • Assume Spanish is secondary. For many of these businesses it is the primary market.

10What we will not do to a client

Each of these is a way SMB marketing money gets wasted, or a way a local business loses its lead flow overnight.

  1. Forty templated city pages. The fastest way to get a small site classified as spam.
  2. Buying, gating or incentivizing reviews. A GBP suspension can end a local business's lead flow overnight.
  3. Broad paid search before the phone gets answered. Fix operations first — ads amplify whatever already exists, including "nobody picks up."
  4. Keyword-stuffed GBP names ("Joe's Plumbing | Best Plumber Austin TX"). It works until the suspension.
  5. Inaccurate GBP categories chosen for volume rather than truth.
  6. Machine-translated Spanish. It signals disrespect to the exact audience it targets.
  7. Reporting impressions to an owner who thinks in jobs. Always dollars, always their numbers.
  8. Promising rankings. Promise a system, a cadence and a scorecard.
  9. Hiding the AI-attribution gap. Say plainly that 35–70% of AI referrals are invisible and that our numbers are floors.
  10. Selling content volume to a client scoring 30/100. Foundation first, every time. Content on a broken foundation is the most common way SMB marketing money is wasted.

11Vertical annexes

Same 90-day system. These are the deltas worth knowing before kickoff.

HVAC · plumbing · electrical

Seasonal demand spikes — pre-load content and ad budget six weeks ahead of season. Emergency intent is high-ticket, so 24 hour, same day and emergency qualifiers matter more than volume terms. Local Services Ads are usually the best first paid dollar. License numbers on-site are a real trust signal. Financing options answer a top AI question ("can I finance a new AC?"). Manufacturer dealer-locator listings — Trane, Carrier, Lennox — are high-authority local links most competitors never claim.

Landscaping · lawn · cleaning

Recurring-revenue framing beats one-off job framing. Before/after photos are the entire content strategy. Neighborhood-level targeting outperforms city-level. Strongly Spanish-language market on both the customer and the hiring side — Moil Smart Hiring plus bilingual content is a genuine combined advantage here.

Restaurants · food

GBP menu, ordering links and photo volume dominate. Reviews arrive fast and cut both ways, so response speed is critical. Restaurant + Menu schema. Instagram and TikTok matter more than blog content. AI answers about food lean heavily on review text and photos, so photo volume is an AEO lever, not a vanity metric.

Salons · barbers · personal care

Booking-link friction is the whole conversion story. Individual stylist pages build person-level E-E-A-T and rank for name searches. Photo reviews outperform text. HairSalon schema plus Service per service with real prices.

Auto repair

service + make/model is a large untapped long-tail (brake repair honda civic austin). Diagnostic question content performs unusually well in AI answers. Warranty and ASE certification signals are strong trust and E-E-A-T markers.

Professional services — accounting, legal, insurance

E-E-A-T is the binding constraint: real credentials, real bios, real bar or CPA numbers. Person schema per practitioner. Higher content depth required than for trades. Bilingual is a major differentiator — and note YMYL sensitivity: accuracy standards are higher and mistakes cost more.
