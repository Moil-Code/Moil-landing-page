## 0. What this is, and what makes it different from Playbook 1

Playbook 1 optimizes one B2B SaaS site for national and bilingual demand. This playbook is a
**delivery system**: the same 90-day sequence, run over and over, for clients who are typically
1–20-person local service businesses — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, landscaping, cleaning, restaurants,
salons, auto, home services — often bilingual, often Hispanic-owned, and almost always with no
marketing plan at all (~50% of small businesses don't have one).

The gap between the two playbooks is one word: **local**.

| | Playbook 1 (Moil) | Playbook 2 (clients) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary battlefield | National SERPs + AI answers | Local Pack + Google Business Profile + AI local answers |
| Biggest single lever | Original research + comparison pages | Google Business Profile + reviews |
| GBP share of ranking signal | n/a | **~32%** of local ranking signals |
| Content volume needed | High (clusters, 47+ posts) | Low (8–15 pages, done properly) |
| Time to first result | 3–6 months | **2–8 weeks** (GBP moves fast) |
| Who executes | Moil growth team | The owner, with Moil + a Moil coach |
| Success metric | Citation share, trials | Calls, form fills, booked jobs |

**Design constraint:** the client is the owner, and the owner has 45 minutes a week. Every deliverable
below either runs inside Moil (Content360, plan, coach, Smart Hiring) or is a one-page checklist. If a
step needs an agency to execute, it doesn't belong in this playbook — that's the whole point of the
$25/mo vs. $3,000–$8,000/mo story.

## 2. Intake and diagnostic (week 0)

### 2.1 Data to collect before anything else

- [ ] Legal business name, exact address, phone, hours (this becomes the NAP standard)
- [ ] Google Business Profile — claimed? verified? who has the login?
- [ ] Website URL, platform, who can edit it
- [ ] Service area: cities, ZIPs, radius
- [ ] Top 3 services by profit (not by volume — by profit)
- [ ] Top 3 local competitors by name
- [ ] Current review count and average rating, per platform
- [ ] Languages the business actually serves customers in
- [ ] Existing analytics: GA4? Search Console? call tracking? anything?
- [ ] What a job is worth (average ticket) and what closes (lead → job rate)

That last item is non-negotiable. Without average ticket and close rate we cannot report in dollars,
and SMB owners rightly disengage from reports denominated in impressions.

### 2.2 The 100-point Local Visibility Scorecard

Score at intake, re-score at day 30 / 60 / 90. This is the client's progress bar and our QA gate.

| Area | Checks | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Business Profile** | Claimed & verified (5) · correct primary category (8) · secondary categories (4) · all services listed w/ descriptions (5) · 10+ real photos (4) · hours incl. holidays (2) · service areas set (3) · products/menu where relevant (2) | **33** |
| **Reviews** | Count vs. 150 threshold (8) · rating ≥4.5 (5) · owner responds to all (4) · recency: ≥2/month (3) | **20** |
| **On-page** | Homepage names city + service in H1 (4) · one page per core service (5) · NAP in footer sitewide (2) · LocalBusiness schema (3) · mobile passes CWV (3) · clear phone CTA above fold (2) | **19** |
| **AEO readiness** | Direct-answer block on homepage (4) · FAQ page + FAQPage schema (4) · service-area page per city (3) · consistent facts everywhere (3) | **14** |
| **Citations / directories** | NAP identical across top 10 directories (5) · Apple Business Connect (2) · Bing Places (2) | **9** |
| **Measurement** | GA4 installed (2) · Search Console verified (2) · call tracking or unique tracked number (1) | **5** |
| | | **100** |

Score bands: **0–35** = broken, fix fundamentals only. **36–60** = typical SMB, big fast wins
available. **61–80** = competitive, push AEO and content. **81+** = defend and expand to adjacent
cities/services.

Most clients score 25–45 at intake. Say so — it's reassuring, not insulting: the fixes are cheap and
the competitors are in the same shape.

## 3. Phase 1 — Foundation (days 1–30)

Goal: stop losing leads the business has already earned. No content, no links, no strategy decks.
Nearly all of the fast win is here.

### 3.1 Google Business Profile (do this first, always)

1. **Claim and verify.** If the login is lost, run reclamation before anything else.
2. **Primary category.** The #1 individual Local Pack factor. Pick the most specific accurate one —
   "HVAC contractor" beats "contractor"; "Mexican restaurant" beats "restaurant". Check what the
   top-3 map competitors use, then pick the one that matches what the business actually does most.
3. **Secondary categories.** Add every accurate one, no stuffing. Inaccurate categories get profiles
   suspended.
4. **Services**, each with a 2–3 sentence description containing the service name and the city. This
   text feeds AI Overviews on local queries directly.
5. **Business description**, 750 chars: what, who for, which cities, languages served, one proof point.
6. **Photos:** 10+ real ones — team, trucks, before/after, storefront, work in progress. No stock.
7. **Hours**, including holiday hours. Wrong hours is the most common cause of a bad first review.
8. **Service areas** set to real cities served, not a 200-mile fantasy radius.
9. **Attributes:** "Spanish spoken," women-owned, veteran-owned, Latino-owned — set every true one.
   These are filterable and they surface in AI answers about the business.
10. **Messaging and booking** on, only if the owner will actually respond within an hour.
11. **Weekly Google Post** — Content360 can generate these from the client's content calendar.

**Bilingual note:** GBP is one profile, one language. Put the primary market's language in the
description, and cover the second language via the "Spanish spoken" attribute, Spanish reviews, and a
Spanish page on the site.

### 3.2 NAP consistency

Even minor Name/Address/Phone discrepancies suppress local rankings. Write the one canonical NAP
string in the client file, then make these 10 match it byte-for-byte:

Google Business Profile · website footer · Apple Business Connect · Bing Places · Facebook · Yelp ·
Better Business Bureau · industry directory (Angi/Thumbtack/HomeAdvisor as relevant) · local Chamber
of Commerce · Hispanic Chamber or local Latino business directory where applicable.

Chamber and EDC listings are doubly valuable — Moil already has those partnerships, so they're a real
delivery advantage, not a cold outreach task.

### 3.3 Review engine (the 150 threshold)

Below roughly 150 reviews per location, AI assistants rarely name a business in local
recommendations. This is the most under-appreciated fact in local AEO, and most SMBs are at 12.

**The system, not the ask:**

- Trigger the request at the moment of delight — job completed, invoice paid, tech leaving.
- One channel, one link. SMS with the direct GBP review link out-converts everything else.
- Ask by name, from a person, in the customer's language.
- **Respond to 100% of reviews within 48 hours.** Responses are indexable text and a ranking signal —
  and the response is where the service name and city legitimately belong.
- Never gate, never incentivize, never bulk-import. A review-gating penalty undoes a year of work.
- Target: **+8–15 reviews/month sustained**, mixed EN/ES where the customer base is mixed.

Math for the client: at 12 reviews and +10/month, they cross 150 in about 14 months — and cross the
"competitive locally" line (≈50) in month four. Set that expectation on day one so month two doesn't
feel like failure.

### 3.4 Website minimum viable structure

Eight pages. Not more, until these eight are genuinely good.

| Page | Must contain |
|---|---|
| Home | H1 with primary service + primary city · 40–60 word direct-answer block · phone CTA above fold · 3 proof points · links to every service page |
| Service page × 3 | One per profitable service. What it is, what it costs (a range beats silence), how long, service area, 3 FAQs, photos of real work |
| About | Real owner name, real photo, years in business, licenses, certifications, languages. This is the E-E-A-T page |
| Service areas | One section or page per city served, with genuinely local content — not a city-name find-and-replace |
| Reviews | Embedded real reviews + link to GBP |
| Contact | NAP, embedded map, hours, form, and a tel: link |
| FAQ | 10 real customer questions, matched 1:1 to `FAQPage` schema |
| Spanish mirror | Where the customer base warrants it — see §6 |

**Schema minimum:** `LocalBusiness` (or the right subtype — `HVACBusiness`, `Plumber`, `Restaurant`,
`HairSalon`), `Service` per service page, `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`. Include `areaServed`,
`openingHours`, `telephone`, `priceRange`, `sameAs` for every profile from §3.2, and
`aggregateRating` **only** if reviews are genuinely displayed on the page.

**Speed:** LCP < 2.5s on mobile, one hero image properly sized, no carousel, no chat widget that
blocks paint. Local buyers are on phones and 81% of AI Overview queries are mobile.

### 3.5 Measurement, installed before the work starts

- GA4 + the AI-Surfaces custom channel group (regex in [Playbook 1 §10.1](./seo-aeo-playbook-moil.md#101-ga4-setup))
- Search Console verified, sitemap submitted
- A tracked phone number, or at minimum GBP call-history plus `tel:` click events as key events
- Baseline snapshot: GBP views/calls/direction requests, review count, current map rank for the top 5
  service+city terms

Without a day-0 baseline there is no month-3 report, and the engagement dies of unprovability.

## 4. Phase 2 — Momentum (days 31–90)

Foundation is live. Now build the surfaces that earn rankings and AI mentions.

### 4.1 Keyword set — small and honest

Local SMBs need about 20 terms, not 2,000. Four shapes:

| Shape | Example | Page |
|---|---|---|
| `service + city` | `ac repair austin` | Service page or service-area section |
| `service + near me` | `emergency plumber near me` | GBP + homepage |
| `service + qualifier` | `24 hour ac repair`, `same day drain cleaning` | Service page section |
| `question` | `how much does ac repair cost in austin`, `why is my ac blowing warm air` | Blog / FAQ |
| `Spanish` | `plomero cerca de mí`, `reparación de aire acondicionado austin` | Spanish page |

Follow khasky's rule: search the term first, look at what Google actually returns, and match the page
type to the SERP. If the SERP is a map pack, the answer is GBP work — not a blog post.

#### Order of operations — questions before volume

Build the client's Q&A list *before* touching a keyword tool. Ask the owner and their techs: **what do
customers actually ask on the phone?** Those questions, in the customer's own words, are the page
structure. They are also almost always higher-intent than anything a volume tool surfaces — a customer
asking "why is my AC blowing warm air and will it cost more than $500" is far closer to booking than one
typing `ac repair`.

The 20-term set above then does exactly one job: choosing between two equally true phrasings, and
catching demand nobody thought to answer. It never sets the agenda. Volume tells you how many people
search a phrase; it cannot tell you which answer books the job. This is the same doctrine
[Playbook 1 §4](./seo-aeo-playbook-moil.md) runs on, and we hold clients to it because it is what works.

**The Q&A build, per page:**

1. **Harvest 8–12 real questions** per service page — from calls, texts, and what techs get asked on
   site. Verbatim, in the customer's words, in both languages where the customer base is mixed.
2. **Answer each in 40–80 words** under a question-shaped `<h2>`, self-contained enough to survive being
   lifted into an AI answer.
3. **State real numbers** — a price range, a timeframe, a warranty length. Vague answers are unciteable.
4. **Reconcile with `FAQPage` schema:** one source of truth between the visible Q&A and the JSON-LD.

### 4.2 Service-area pages without doorway spam

The temptation is 40 city pages from one template. Google's helpful-content systems target exactly
that, and it fails.

**The rule: 5 real pages beat 40 templated ones.** A service-area page earns its URL only if it has
≥3 of: a named local job or case study, a photo taken in that city, a neighborhood or landmark
reference that's actually true, city-specific pricing or permit/code notes, a review from a customer
there, or a genuine local nuance (hard water, coastal humidity, HOA rules, seasonal load).

Cadence: one city page per month, best city first. Expand only after the first ranks.

### 4.3 Content — 4 pieces a month, generated in Moil

Content360 produces the 30-day calendar; the owner approves and adds the one thing AI can't:
photos of real work and one specific detail from a real job.

| Slot | Type | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost/pricing guide (`how much does X cost in {city}`) | Highest-intent question searches; the most-cited local content type |
| 2 | Problem/diagnostic post (`why is my X doing Y`) | Top-of-funnel + genuinely useful in AI answers |
| 3 | Job story / before-after with real photos | Proof, E-E-A-T, GBP post, and social in one shoot |
| 4 | Seasonal or local post | Freshness and local relevance |

Plus: one GBP post weekly, and the month's reviews responded to.

**Don't let every client look identical.** Flyer-style AI posts are effortless and they flood social —
which is exactly the problem. Used daily they make every brand look the same, and that generic look is
what Content360's varied templates exist to avoid. Two working rules: **a flyer invites an action, daily
content is an image plus a caption** — don't use one for the other; and rotate genuinely different
templates (Addy has 40+) rather than reusing one. The differentiator no competitor can copy is the
client's own photos of their own work, so every piece should carry at least one.

**Publish the pricing content.** Owners resist it. The counter-argument: AI assistants answer "how much
does ac repair cost in Austin" constantly, and they cite the businesses that state ranges. Silence
means a competitor's number gets quoted instead of ours.

### 4.4 Local AEO — how an SMB gets named by ChatGPT and Gemini

Local AI recommendations resolve mostly from **entity strength, reviews, and directory consensus** —
not from clever on-page copy. In priority order:

1. **Review volume and recency.** The ~150 threshold. This is the dominant local AEO lever.
2. **Directory consensus.** The same NAP, category and services repeated across 10+ sources is what
   the model reads as "this business exists and does this."
3. **Consistent facts.** Hours, phone, services, and languages identical everywhere. Contradiction
   → the model declines to state the fact.
4. **Extractable on-page answers.** 40–60 word direct answers under question-shaped H2s. Service +
   city named explicitly in the first sentence.
5. **Third-party mentions.** Local news, Chamber pages, supplier "find a dealer" pages, local blogs,
   local subreddit and Facebook-group mentions.
6. **`LocalBusiness` schema** that matches the visible page exactly.

**Skip for most clients:** `llms.txt`. No measurable lift, and an SMB's 40 minutes a week is better
spent on reviews. Add it only for clients with a real content library.

**Client AEO test, monthly, 10 minutes.** Ask each engine, in both languages where relevant:

- "Best {service} in {city}"
- "Who should I call for {problem} in {city}?"
- "{Service} in {city} that speaks Spanish"
- "Is {Business Name} any good?"
- "How much does {service} cost in {city}?"

Log: named or not, what it said, who else got named. This screenshot is the single most persuasive
slide in the monthly report — and when a client first sees themselves named, retention takes care of
itself.

### 4.5 Paid, only where it earns its keep

Most of these clients should not run broad paid search. Three exceptions:

1. **Google Local Services Ads** (where the vertical is eligible — HVAC, plumbing, electrical,
   cleaning, locksmith, etc.). Pay per lead, Google-guaranteed badge, sits above the map pack. Almost
   always the best first paid dollar for a service business.
2. **Brand + competitor-brand search**, tiny budget, only once the site converts.
3. **Emergency/urgent terms** where the job is high-ticket and same-day (`emergency ac repair`).
   Bid these during season only.

Everything else — display, broad awareness campaigns, boosted posts — is where SMB ad budgets die.
Say no on the client's behalf. And per khasky: never use remarketing to mask a bad offer or a phone
nobody answers.

## 6. Bilingual delivery (Moil's structural advantage)

There are 5M+ Hispanic-owned US businesses generating ~$800B/yr, and Latino-owned employer businesses
grew 35% from 2019–2024 vs. 6% nationally. Almost no local marketing provider serves them in Spanish
properly. This is the most defensible part of the client offer.

**Do:**
- Real Spanish URLs — `/es/servicios` or `/servicios`, with reciprocal `hreflang` and `x-default`.
- Spanish keyword research from scratch. `plomero` vs `fontanero` vs `plomería` varies by region and
  community; ask the client which word their customers use, then verify against actual search results.
- The GBP "Spanish spoken" attribute set, and a Spanish sentence in the business description where the
  customer base is majority Spanish-speaking.
- Solicit reviews in the customer's language. Spanish reviews are what make a business surface for
  "que hable español" queries — in both classic search and AI answers.
- Spanish content in the Content360 calendar from month one, not as a later phase.

**Don't:**
- Machine-translate and ship. Bad Spanish reads as untrustworthy to exactly the audience it targets.
- Serve translated content on the same URL by cookie or JS swap. There must be a distinct, crawlable
  URL per language, or neither version ranks.
- Assume Spanish is secondary. For many of these businesses it's the primary market.

## 7. Reporting — one page, in dollars

SMB owners disengage from dashboards. Every month, one page, five blocks:

```
1. LEADS THIS MONTH        calls · forms · GBP messages · direction requests
                           vs. last month, vs. baseline

2. WHERE THEY CAME FROM    Google Maps · organic · AI assistants · direct · paid
                           (footnote: 35–70% of AI referrals show as Direct — this is a floor)

3. VISIBILITY              map rank for the top 5 service+city terms
                           review count vs. the 150 AI-citation threshold
                           Local Visibility Scorecard: {n}/100, was {n}

4. AI ANSWER CHECK         "Best {service} in {city}" → named? screenshot
                           what the assistant said about the business

5. NEXT MONTH              three things we're doing, one thing we need from you
```

Then the sentence that matters: *"{n} leads × your {x}% close rate × ${y} average ticket =
$~{z} in attributable work."* Using the client's own numbers from §2.1.

**Expectation-setting script for month 1:** "GBP changes often move within 2–4 weeks. Website and
review work compounds over 3–6 months. Anyone promising page-one in 30 days is selling you the map
pack you already own for free, or lying."

## 10. Anti-patterns — what we will not do to a client

1. **Forty templated city pages.** The fastest way to get a small site classified as spam.
2. **Buying reviews, gating reviews, or incentivizing reviews.** A GBP suspension can end a local
   business's lead flow overnight.
3. **Broad paid search before the phone gets answered.** Fix operations first; ads amplify whatever
   already exists, including "nobody picks up."
4. **Keyword-stuffed GBP names** ("Joe's Plumbing | Best Plumber Austin TX"). It works until the
   suspension.
5. **Inaccurate GBP categories** chosen for volume rather than truth.
6. **Machine-translated Spanish.** It signals disrespect to the exact audience it targets.
7. **Reporting impressions to an owner who thinks in jobs.** Always dollars, always their numbers.
8. **Promising rankings.** Promise a system, a cadence, and a scorecard.
9. **Hiding the AI-attribution gap.** Say plainly that 35–70% of AI referrals are invisible and that
   our numbers are floors.
10. **Selling content volume to a client scoring 30/100.** Foundation first, every time. Content on a
    broken foundation is the most common way SMB marketing money is wasted.

## 11. Vertical annexes

Same 90-day system; these are the deltas worth knowing before kickoff.

### HVAC / plumbing / electrical
Seasonal demand spikes — pre-load content and ad budget 6 weeks ahead of season. Emergency intent is
high-ticket: `24 hour`, `same day`, `emergency` qualifiers matter more than volume terms. Local
Services Ads are usually the best first paid dollar. License numbers on-site are a real trust signal.
Financing options answer a top AI question ("can I finance a new AC?"). Manufacturer dealer-locator
listings (Trane, Carrier, Lennox) are high-authority local links most competitors never claim.

### Landscaping / lawn / cleaning
Recurring-revenue framing beats one-off job framing. Before/after photos are the entire content
strategy. Neighborhood-level targeting outperforms city-level. Strongly Spanish-language market on
both the customer and the hiring side — Moil Smart Hiring plus bilingual content is a genuine combined
advantage here.

### Restaurants / food
GBP menu, ordering links, and photo volume dominate. Reviews arrive fast and cut both ways; response
speed is critical. `Restaurant` + `Menu` schema. Instagram/TikTok matter more than blog content. AI
answers about food lean heavily on review text and photos, so photo volume is an AEO lever, not a
vanity metric.

### Salons / barbers / personal care
Booking-link friction is the whole conversion story. Individual stylist pages build person-level
E-E-A-T and rank for name searches. Photo reviews outperform text. `HairSalon` schema + `Service` per
service with real prices.

### Auto repair
`service + make/model` is a large untapped long-tail (`brake repair honda civic austin`). Diagnostic
question content performs unusually well in AI answers. Warranty and certification (ASE) signals are
strong trust and E-E-A-T markers.

### Professional services (accounting, legal, insurance)
E-E-A-T is the binding constraint: real credentials, real bios, real bar/CPA numbers. `Person` schema
per practitioner. Higher content depth required than for trades. Bilingual is a major differentiator —
and note YMYL sensitivity: accuracy standards are higher and mistakes cost more.
