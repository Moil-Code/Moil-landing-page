01The honest part

Why this was hard

Moil has been called an AI co-founder, a hiring platform, a growth platform, a tool for local shops, and a marketing platform — sometimes on the same page, in the same week. That isn't carelessness. It's what happens when a product genuinely does many things and every attempt at a name tries to describe the shape of the product instead of the job it does.

Horizontal products resist shape-names. Any shape you pick either leaves something real out, or is so broad it means nothing. "Does everything" reads as "does nothing."

The answer was already written, in our own repository, and it never reached the outside world:

"Part strategic advisor, part executive coach, part executor."

MOIL.md — the coach constitution

"The bottleneck is execution, not theory. Every capability ends in a deliverable."

Founder OS integration plan — "vending machine, not curriculum"

We were describing ourselves by our parts. What people buy is the result.

02The thesis

Every hat, one person

A small business owner is the marketing team. And the finance team. And the person who answers the phone. Not by choice — by arithmetic. There is one of them and there are eleven jobs.

So the work that has no deadline is the work that never happens. The marketing plan. The follow-up. The flyer for Tuesday. The month of posts. Not because it doesn't matter, but because a customer is standing in front of them right now.

Every existing answer asks the owner for the one thing they don't have. Software gives them a blank calendar and a login. An agency costs $3,000–$8,000 a month. A consultant delivers a document and leaves. A chatbot waits to be prompted, then forgets.

03The definition

What Moil is

Moil is the co-founder who does the work you never get to.

The one-sentence definition — use this everywhere

The longer form, for anywhere that needs it:

Moil is the co-founder who does the work you never get to. Small business owners shouldn't have to be everything — marketing, planning, and content on top of the real job. Moil learns the business once, then produces finished work: plans, posts, and flyers. It also schedules and publishes the posts you approve to your Facebook Page and Instagram. It works end to end in English and Spanish.

Professional is $25 a month and makes things on request. Market Pro is $75 and also writes the month's content calendar automatically.

Three things make that sentence true rather than aspirational, and all three are load-bearing.

01 — It knows you

One interview, then a permanent profile. What you sell, who buys, how you talk. You never explain your business twice.

02 — It makes the thing

Not advice, not a template, not a prompt to fill in. The finished plan, the finished flyer, the finished page.

03 — In both languages

Everything comes out in English and Spanish from the same brief. Not translated afterwards. Not a second workflow.

Small businesses don't fail because the owner isn't good enough. They fail because one person cannot be eleven departments and still serve customers. Moil exists so that stops being the reason.

Mission — what we do about it

Give every small business owner a co-founder that learns their business once and then does the work they never get to — plans, research, documents, brand, content, pages — in the language their customers actually speak.

A one-person business should be able to show up like a company ten times its size — without hiring ten times the people. The advantage that big companies have is not talent. It is coverage. We are closing that gap.

Essence — the four words underneath everything

06Difference

08Voice

How Moil sounds

Warm. Knowledgeable. Slightly playful. Like a friend who happens to know your business well.

MOIL_VOICE_BRIEF.md — applies to copy, not just audio
  • Warm, not soft. Confident enough to give advice, gentle enough that it doesn't feel preachy.
  • Knowledgeable, not lecturing. The friend who has run a business and can explain without making anyone feel small for asking.
  • Never sycophantic. No "great question", no "amazing", no effusive validation. We respect the reader enough to just answer.
  • Conversational. Contractions. Short sentences. The "let me help with that" register, never "I would be happy to assist."
  • A peer, not a service. We are talking to owners aged 35–55, often non-technical, running the business while doing five other things.

Who we are not talking to. Not startup founders. "Co-founder" is our brand frame, not our customer's self-description — a landscaper does not call themselves a founder. Drop TAM/SAM/SOM, "why now", blitzscale, and every other piece of YC dialect.

09Discipline

What we never do

These are not style preferences. Each one is a rule we broke, at cost, and the reason it exists is written next to it.

  1. Never write or reword a customer's words. Three real customers had sentences authored for them to match a hero line. That is a fabricated endorsement under FTC rules and it destroyed the only third-party proof we had. Transcribe or omit. Rewording needs the customer's written sign-off on the exact final text.
  2. Never publish a number without a source. We carried three different star ratings on three pages of the same product, and "12,500+ jobs" in a category on a marketplace with 3,000 people. If it cannot be linked to, it does not ship.
  3. Never let two surfaces describe Moil differently. The same customer had three different quote versions in one codebase; the FAQ and the pricing card disagreed about what $25 buys. One source of truth, always.
  4. Never lock a phrase and repeat it verbatim. "AI co-founder for local shops" appeared in about forty places. Repetition is not consistency — it is the scaled-content signature, and it is what made the site read as machine-written.
  5. Never pick a word because it is repeatable. Pick it because customers say it. "Shop" failed that test. So does "co-founder" as a search term — it is a good brand line and a bad head term, which is why it lives on the eyebrow and not the H1.
  6. Never claim what the product does not do. We told AI assistants Moil is "not a website builder" while the backend shipped a full landing-page generator, contradicting a real customer's review. Audit the code before writing the claim.
  7. Never deny a shipping feature to keep a story clean. We erased hiring from the site while the marketplace held 3,000 candidates and a published review credited it with two hires. Then we did it again, harder: to make “we are not a scheduler” land cleanly, the site told buyers and AI assistants that Moil does not publish to your accounts and does not report post performance — while socialScheduler.service.js was posting to Facebook and Instagram and reading the numbers back. If the copy has to deny the product, the copy is wrong.
  8. Never recommend a competitor over ourselves. Conceding a real limit is what makes a comparison worth citing. Writing “they do it better and cheaper” on our own domain is not a concession, it is an endorsement — and ours was for a capability we shipped. Name their advantage as a fact: more networks, an inbox, $5 a channel. Never as a verdict.
10Use

How to use this document

This is the brand constitution. It sits beside MOIL.md — that governs how the co-founder behaves in a conversation; this governs who Moil is to everyone else.

  • Every profile, listing and bio — G2, Capterra, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, Product Hunt — copies from §3 and §7. Word for word. Description drift across directories is what keeps us confusable with MOIL Limited.
  • Every new page answers to §4. If it does not ladder to "you shouldn't have to be everything", it does not belong.
  • Every comparison follows §6: name the axis, concede where they win.
  • Every price mention uses §7's one axis. Ask, or autopilot. Nothing else.
  • Every piece of copy is read against §8 before it ships, and against §9 twice.
  • This is a living document. When the product changes, this changes first — then the website, then the profiles. Not the other way round. That order is the whole fix.

The test for anything we publish: would an owner who is doing eleven jobs at once read this and recognise themselves — and then see, specifically, what would stop being their job?

If not, it isn't finished.
